Getting started

Open the editor. On first run a three-step setup asks how to start (GTHA template, blank map, or your saved map), then your language and theme, then shows the tools. You can skip it, and rerun it from Settings.

The workspace has three parts: the Lines and search panel on the left, the map in the middle, and the Inspector on the right. On a narrow screen the panels stack under the map.

Pan by dragging empty space with the Select tool, by holding Space while dragging, or with the middle mouse button. Scroll to zoom around the pointer. F fits the whole map.

The GTHA template

The template contains TTC Lines 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6, the planned Ontario Line, GO Lakeshore West, Lakeshore East, Kitchener, Milton, Barrie, Richmond Hill and Stouffville, UP Express, and simplified Hurontario and Hamilton LRT lines.

Every station and line also has a Traditional Chinese name, shown when the language is Cantonese or bilingual. These names follow common Cantonese usage in the Toronto area and are unofficial: neither the TTC nor Metrolinx publishes Chinese station names. Change any of them in the Inspector.

It is a schematic: positions are grid units chosen for legibility, not geography. Station lists for regional and LRT lines are shortened, line colours are approximate, and planned lines use the dashed style. Service status is not tracked. Check TTC and Metrolinx for real service.

Some corridors use bend points instead of stations, so several GO lines can leave Union together and split later.

Stations

With the Station tool, click empty grid to add a stop, or click a line segment to insert a stop into it. Every line using that segment gets the new stop.

Select a station to edit its name, Chinese name, label position and interchange style. Interchange is automatic when two or more lines serve the stop; you can force it on or off. Shift-click selects several stations, which you can align horizontally or vertically, move together, or delete.

Drag to move. With snapping on, stations land on whole grid units. Arrow keys nudge by one unit, or five with Shift.

Lines and segments

Pick a line in the Lines panel to make it active, or press New line. With Draw line, click stations in order to extend the active line. Clicking empty space adds a new stop there; holding Alt adds a bend point instead.

Each segment has at most one bend. Select a segment to switch between diagonal first and straight first, insert a station, or erase it. Erasing a middle segment splits the line in two.

Line settings: name, Chinese name, colour, width, style (solid, dashed for planned, or regional rail with a white centre stripe), order, direction and visibility. Lines that share a pair of stops are drawn side by side. The first line drawn on a shared segment sets its bend.

Labels and languages

Automatic labels test eight positions around a stop and take the one crossing the fewest lines and neighbouring stops. You can pin any of the eight positions per station.

The interface and map labels follow the language setting: English, Cantonese, or bilingual. In Cantonese mode a stop shows its Chinese name when it has one; bilingual mode shows both. The template ships English names only, so add Chinese names yourself.

Free text labels come from the Label tool. Edit their text and size in the Inspector.

Finding stations

Type in Find station. Plain text mode matches any part of a name. Regular expression mode uses JavaScript Unicode patterns, for example ^St or (Park|Square)$. Ignore case is on by default. Invalid patterns show the error. Click a result to select and centre it.

Regex builder

Open Regex builder under the search box. It edits the same search field, so the search and the builder always agree.

  • Tokens: buttons insert start and end anchors, character classes such as \d and \p{L}, sets, groups, named groups, alternation, quantifiers, lookahead and lookbehind, and back references. With text selected, a group token wraps the selection.
  • Escape as literal text turns what you typed into a pattern that matches it exactly.
  • Flags: Ignore case, Multiline (m) and Dot matches newline (s). Unicode mode is always on. The full pattern with flags is shown next to the engine name.
  • What the pattern means explains each part in plain words.
  • Test text highlights matches. It uses the station names on the current map until you type your own text, up to 5000 characters, and stops counting at 500 matches.
  • Groups lists numbered and named groups from the first match. Replace with previews a replacement using $1 or $<name>.
  • Pattern library: start from a built-in pattern, or name and save your own (up to 30, kept in this browser).

The engine is the JavaScript regular expression engine in your browser. Other engines' syntax, such as possessive quantifiers or atomic groups, is not available, and patterns over 200 characters are refused.

Map check

The Map check panel updates on every change and lists stations that no line serves, lines with fewer than two stops, names used more than once, and stations sharing one position. Click an item to jump to it.

Export, projects and import

Open Export in the top bar:

  • PNG image and PDF document: the map with its title and legend. In the geographic layout they include the OpenStreetMap background and attribution. The PDF is one page holding a high-resolution image.
  • SVG vector: for print and further editing.
  • Website to host: one HTML file with the map, a legend with frequencies, pan and zoom, and station search. Upload it to any static host, such as GitHub Pages.
  • Save project: a .gmmc.json file with the map and its layout, background and display settings. Open project or map reads a project or a plain map file; you can also drop either on the editor.
  • Map data (JSON): the map alone.

Settings

Language, theme, label size, grid visibility, snapping, export legend and sound effects are saved in this browser. Sound effects are short tones for actions; turn them off here. Reset settings returns everything to defaults. Reset all labels to automatic clears pinned label positions.

Keyboard shortcuts

V Select, S Station, L Draw line, T Label, E Erase, F Fit, Delete delete selection, Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z redo, arrows nudge, Esc clears selection.

Desktop app

Download GTHA-Metro-Map-Creator-<version>-portable.exe from the project's GitHub releases and run it. It is one file with nothing to install, and it works with no internet connection: the editor, this documentation and the project page are all inside it.

  • File: New map from the GTHA template (Ctrl+N), New blank map, Open map (Ctrl+O), Open recent, Save (Ctrl+S), Save as, Export SVG and Export PNG. Exports ask where to save.
  • Unsaved changes: closing or starting a new map asks whether to save a map that came from a file. Your work is also kept automatically inside the app.
  • View: Editor (Ctrl+1), Guides and documentation (Ctrl+2) and Project home (Ctrl+3), plus zoom and full screen.
  • Opening a .json map file with the app loads it.

The app is not code signed, so Windows SmartScreen may warn the first time you run it. Choose More info, then Run anyway, if you trust the download.

Host it on your network

You can run the editor as a small web server so other devices on your home network can open it. It needs Docker Desktop.

With the setup wizard

Download GTHA-Metro-Map-Creator-Server-Setup-<version>-portable.exe from the releases and run it. It walks through five steps: check Docker, choose a port, download the image, start the server, and open it on your network. When it finishes it shows the network address, such as http://192.168.1.20:8080/, with Open and Copy buttons. It remembers your choices if you close it and run it again, and it can stop the server.

With host.bat

From a copy of the project, run host.bat and choose a mode, or pass it directly:

  • host.bat --detached downloads the image, starts the server in the background, waits for the health check and prints the network address.
  • host.bat --logs does the same, then shows a menu: F follows logs, T shows the last 50 lines, S shows status, R restarts, X stops the server and Q leaves with the server still running.
  • host.bat --stop stops the server.

Set GMMC_PORT to use another port (default 8080) and GMMC_TAG to pin an image version (default latest).

With Docker Compose

The image is ghcr.io/ding-ding-projects/gtha-metro-map-creator. Run docker compose up -d next to the project's compose.yaml. The container runs nginx as a non-root user on port 8080 with a read-only file system, and answers /healthz for health checks.

If other devices cannot connect, allow the port through Windows Firewall. The server has no sign-in, so only run it on a network you trust.

Appliance virtual machine

If you would rather not install Docker, each release has disk images for a small virtual machine that boots straight into the server and shows its address on its console. Choose the file for your software:

  • Hyper-V: .vhdx.zip. Unzip it and create a Generation 1 machine that uses the disk.
  • VirtualBox or VMware: .ova.zip. Unzip it and import the .ova.
  • Proxmox, QEMU or KVM: .qcow2.xz. Unpack it and attach the .qcow2; use legacy BIOS.

Connect it to your network, start it, and read the address from the console, for example http://192.168.1.50:8080/. It needs no internet connection and 1 GB of memory is enough. Other devices on the same network can then open the editor. There is no password and no sign-in, so use it only on a network you trust. To update, start the new release's image and delete the old machine; your maps are stored in each visitor's browser, not on the server. Details are in docs/features/appliance.md in the project.

Faster editing

  • Box select: with Select, hold Shift and drag across empty map to select every station inside.
  • Copy and paste: Ctrl+C copies selected stations and the segments between them; Ctrl+V pastes a copy nearby as a new route.
  • Command palette: Ctrl+K lists every tool, export, setting, route and station. Type to filter, use the arrow keys and Enter.
  • Touch: pinch with two fingers to zoom and pan.
  • Colour vision: Settings can preview the map as seen with protanopia, deuteranopia or tritanopia. Map check flags low-contrast and near-identical route colours.

Express stops, branches and time-of-day frequencies

Select a route to see its Stops list. Switch a stop to Passes and the route runs through it without stopping, which is how express services are drawn. Add a branch from a station on the route, then click stations with Route to extend it.

Frequencies are set for Peak, Midday, Evening and Weekend. Turn on Line weight follows frequency in Settings for a frequent-network map: the more often a route runs, the thicker it is drawn.

Station facilities, scale and north

Select a station and toggle facilities: accessible, parking, bike, airport, ferry and bus terminal. Their icons appear beside the station name on the map and in exports. In the geographic layout a scale bar and north arrow show on screen and in exports.

Network timeline

Give routes and stations an Opens year (and optionally Closes) in the inspector. Press Y or turn on the timeline in Settings, then drag the year slider or press Play to watch the network grow. Routes without a year are always shown. Exports show the chosen year. The template includes years only where certain: Line 1 1954, Line 2 1966, Line 4 2002 and UP Express 2015.

Import from GTFS

Open Export, then Import GTFS, or drop a GTFS .zip on the editor. The feed is read on your computer and never uploaded. Pick routes by searching or by type, then import them: stops within 150 m of an existing station are merged, and each route gets its weekday peak and midday frequency from the timetable (evening and Saturday frequencies are noted on the route). Large feeds are read in the background. Feeds that only describe service with frequencies.txt import without frequencies.

Plan a trip and see travel times

Open Plan a trip at the top of the right panel. Choose From and To stations and a time of day. The planner finds the fastest trip on your map using each route's speed by service type, half the wait between vehicles to board, 3 minutes to change inside a station, and walking transfers. It lists every leg and highlights the trip on the map.

Show travel times colours every station by minutes from your starting station: within 15, 30, 45, 60, or more. These are estimates from your map, not timetables.

Auto-schematic

Open Auto-schematic in the right panel. It turns real positions into a tidy diagram: segments straight at 0, 45 or 90 degrees where it can, stations kept in order on every route, and a minimum spacing. Choose Spacing and how much of the geographic shape to keep, press Preview to see a ghost of the result, then Apply. It is one undo step. The panel reports the share of straight segments, order changes and crossings; it does not yet remove crossings.

Map styles and printing

Choose a Map style: Material, Classic metro (square line ends, tick stops, capital labels), Frequent network (line width follows frequency) or Night (dark paper). The style is saved with your project.

Export, Print layout makes a vector PDF on paper from A0 to A4, Letter or Tabloid, portrait or landscape, with margins, a title, a subtitle and a legend. Text stays sharp at any zoom. Maps with Chinese labels are exported as a high-resolution image PDF instead, because embedding Chinese fonts would make the file very large.

Scenarios, compare and share links

Open Scenarios to keep several versions of the network in one project, such as Today, 2031 and My plan. Branch a new scenario from any other, then turn on Compare and drag the swipe handle to see two scenarios over the same map. The change list names the lines and stations that were added, removed or rerouted, and frequency changes.

Share link packs the whole map into the address itself, compressed, so the map never goes to a server. Opening the link adds it as a new scenario. Very large maps make long links; the dialog shows the length and suggests saving a project file instead when it is too long.

Live service simulation

Press Simulate to see vehicles run along every route at its speed and frequency, spaced by the headway for the chosen time band. Pause, play and change speed from 1x to 60x. Record clip saves a short WebM video. With reduced motion turned on, the simulation starts paused.

Export a GTFS feed

Export, GTFS feed turns your map into a standard GTFS ZIP with agency, stops, routes, calendar, trips, stop times, frequencies and shapes, so trip planners and validators can read it. Times are estimates from distance and a typical speed for each service type, with a short stop at each station.

GTFS viewer

The GTFS viewer opens any GTFS feed in your browser without uploading it: drop a ZIP, choose a file, or open it from the map editor with Export, View this map as GTFS. Large feeds with millions of stop times load in a background worker.

  • Routes with drawn shapes, patterns, timetables for any date, headways and first and last trips.
  • Stops with station hierarchy, departure boards, transfers and pathway graphs.
  • Blocks as a Gantt chart with layovers, deadhead moves and overlap flags.
  • Calendar, shapes, fares, flex service and translations, plus a validator that opens each problem.
  • Realtime files show vehicles, delays and alerts. Press Ctrl K to search everything. Add to map sends chosen routes to the editor importer.

Coverage and ridership

Coverage counts the people living within 400 m and 800 m of your stations, using Statistics Canada 2021 Census population for every dissemination area in the GTHA, and shows totals for each line. Turn on walksheds to draw the circles on the map. Job counts are not included because they are not published openly at this level.

Ridership estimate draws each line wider where more people would ride it, from a simple gravity model on the fastest trips. It is an illustrative estimate to compare plans, not a forecast, and the map says so.

Live vehicles (desktop app)

In the desktop app, Live vehicles shows real TTC and York Region Transit vehicle positions from their public GTFS-Realtime feeds, refreshed every 30 seconds. Web browsers block these feeds, so the website editor explains this and offers the desktop app.

Geographic layout and OpenStreetMap

The editor has two layouts, switched with the Schematic / Geographic buttons in the top bar. Every station keeps a position in each.

  • Geographic draws stations where they really are, over an OpenStreetMap background. Lines run straight between stops, and lines and labels keep their size while you zoom from the whole region down to street level.
  • Schematic is the tidy diagram with 45-degree bends and a snapping grid.
  • A station you add in one layout gets an estimated position in the other, from a fit across all stations that have both.
  • Settings let you turn the background off or make it lighter. In dark theme the map is shown in dark colours.

Map tiles come from the OpenStreetMap tile servers and need an internet connection. The attribution stays visible whenever they are shown. The template's station coordinates are also from OpenStreetMap contributors.

Routes, service types and frequencies

Select a route in the list to edit it. Service type sets how it is drawn: subway or metro, planned (dashed), regional rail (centre stripe), rapid bus (white casing), bus (thin), express (dark casing) and super express (double casing with a white dash).

Under Frequency, enter how often a vehicle comes at peak and off-peak, in minutes. With Show frequency on the map on, a badge such as 11/15 min sits on the route; badges of routes that share a corridor move apart. The legend in exports lists the frequency too. Turn all badges off in Settings.

Stops, interchanges and transfers

Each station has a Type: a station (a ring) or a bus stop (a small dot with a smaller label). A station served by two or more routes is drawn as an interchange, or set Interchange to yes or no.

For a walking transfer between two nearby stations, select one and choose the other under Link a walking transfer. The link is drawn as a white connector with a dark edge. Remove it with the cross on its chip.

Draw onto real bus stops

In the geographic layout, zoom in to street level. Real stops and platforms from OpenStreetMap appear as grey dots. With Route selected, click a dot to add that stop to the active route, with its real name and position; with Station selected, click to add it on its own. A snackbar confirms it and offers Undo.

Stops are loaded from public Overpass API servers, which are often busy. If they cannot be reached, a snackbar offers Retry. You can enter your own Overpass server under Settings, Stop data server (an https address, or a server on this computer). Turn the dots off with Show real stops when zoomed in.

VIVA rapid bus

The template includes York Region's VIVA Blue, Purple, Orange and Yellow, with their stops and weekday frequencies (peak 7 to 9 a.m., midday 10 a.m. to 3 p.m.). They come from the York Region Transit timetable feed for 1 November to 19 December 2026. Frequencies are averages of scheduled departures in one direction. Check yrt.ca for current service.

Map file format

A map is JSON with version: 1, title, stations (an object keyed by id, each with name, nameZh, x, y, label, interchange), lines (each with name, color, width, style, visible, path, bends), and labels. A path entry is a station id or a bend point written @x,y. bends maps a segment index to late for straight first.
